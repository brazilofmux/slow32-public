# State of the COBOL, 2026-10-03

Three days after `state-2026-09-30.md`, which stays as the record of the
first month.  This one covers what those days were about: the road to
HIR (cobol ISSUES-123, `plans/census.md`, `plans/hir.md`), and an
assessment of where the compiler stands, made the evening the work
reached a stopping point.

## In one paragraph

s32-cobc's hot statements now go through an optimizing back end.  A
census decides which numeric items can live as machine words; the
statements over them -- arithmetic, moves, compares, IF, EVALUATE, the
in-line PERFORMs, GO TO within a performed range, DISPLAY, numeric
editing -- are lowered to stage08's HIR (SSA, the optimizer, a
graph-colouring register allocator) as *islands* inside the text the
compiler has always emitted, and a PERFORM of a paragraph whose
statements are all lowerable is emitted as those statements in place.
The verbs that are runtime work -- I/O, SORT, STRING, INSPECT, SEARCH,
CALL -- stay as the text emitter wrote them and take part in an island as
opaque nodes.  Every program can still be compiled entirely the old way
(`-fno-hir`), and generators compare the two on every run.

## What was built, in order

- **The census** (steps 1-3): which items stand alone, which PERFORMs are
  procedures.  Items that nothing reaches except by name are kept in the
  machine's form.
- **Islands** (step 4): COMPUTE, ADD, SUBTRACT, MULTIPLY, DIVIDE with
  REMAINDER, numeric MOVE, relations with AND/OR/NOT, in-line PERFORM
  VARYING/UNTIL; the arithmetic is the runtime's store rule written out,
  each step skipped where the bounds prove it does nothing.
- Items in storage, the integer functions, alphanumeric MOVEs and
  compares of known lengths, DISPLAY.
- **Text statements inside islands**: a verb nobody lowers is a node
  whose code is the text emitter's own lines.  An island stores and
  reloads around such a node only the items the node can touch -- a
  native item is reached by its label or not at all, so the lines say.
- **PERFORM as its paragraph's nodes**, bounded by expanded size;
  EVALUATE as an IF chain; GO TO as a branch inside an inlined range;
  index-names; reference modification with computed positions; subscripted
  items and native tables; DISPLAY and packed stores in line; numeric
  editing in line for the common pictures.
- MULH and MULHU in HIR (and upstream in stage08, and in Fortran's copy),
  so a 64-bit product is instructions.

The real workload: majesty's csv2fw converts 2.6 M bytes of CSV in
0.18 s where it took 0.27 (3.89 G instructions to 3.35 G); its per-byte
path -- the READ loop, the paragraph it performs and those that one
performs -- is one island of about 94 instructions a byte where the
text ran about 400.  The month-end batch, 26 programs, runs in under
half a second.  The report programs themselves did not move: their time
is the runtime's (SORT, the line reads and writes), where generated code
is an eighth of the instructions.

Two things measured and not built: READ and WRITE as direct calls (two
instructions a record), and islands for runs inside performed paragraphs
without inlining (at par at best).

## What the differentials found

Three axes are compared on every run: islands against text, native items
against items as written, and both against an oracle where one exists.
When two of our own paths disagreed, the oracle or arithmetic decided --
and several times it sided with the new path against the old:

- the runtime counted a packed item's pad nibble as a digit;
- the text's word arithmetic wrapped through a MOD over a dividend wider
  than a word;
- a MOVE to several receivers re-identified its sender after the first.

And in the islands themselves, each caught by a generator seed or a
harness test before it was committed: a text node carrying registers the
island owned, the sign of a truncated value read at the wrong point, a
statement taken for the PERFORM inside it.  The record is in ISSUES-123's
Twelfth to Eighteenth entries, with the seed where a generator found it.

Outside the compiler the same work found: the DBT never chained the exit
of a block that reached its length limit (AArch64; DBT-22), an x86-64
float-to-unsigned conversion wrong from 2^31 up (DBT-23, found on the
x86-64 validation run), and the cross compilers dropping the high word of
a 64-bit global initializer (selfhost ISSUES-80).

## Where it stands

It runs real ledgers byte-identically and quickly, and it has a method
that catches its own mistakes.  That is the claim, and it is checkable:
CCVS-85's 348 programs match the oracle; the month-end's outputs match
their references; the generators agree across both policies on fresh
seeds.

What it does not have, said as plainly:

- **Exposure.**  A sweep on fresh seeds still finds something now and
  then.  Compilers earn trust by years in the field; this one earns it by
  oracles and generators, which is sound and young.  The sweep should be
  routine, not an event.
- **Coverage at the edges**, all listed in `plans/hir.md`: editing with
  `*`, `B 0 /`, BLANK WHEN ZERO, the locale clauses, more than nine
  digits, de-editing; items past 18 digits in islands.  Content a picture
  does not describe (characters in a COMP item after a group MOVE) is
  nobody's promise, and the generators keep their items numeric.
- **Tooling.**  No symbolic debugger; an island's code has no source
  lines inside it (`-fprofile-lines` labels the text, not the HIR).
- **Scope.**  COBOL 85 is complete and 2002 substantial.  Object
  orientation is out by decision.  JSON and XML GENERATE/PARSE are not
  there; GENERATE would come first if a program wants it.

## Direction

Use it, and let real work say what gets fixed.  The optimization items
left are small and listed, and none is a prerequisite for anything.  The
gates stay on every commit: the harness, majesty's batch and functions,
the Open Systems papers, CCVS, the runtime built by the self-hosted
compiler, and the generators at both policies.
