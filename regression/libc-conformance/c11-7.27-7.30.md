# C11 7.27-7.30 Time, wide characters: the two C libraries

> Status 2026-11-01: 40 probes (`probes/7.27*.c`, `7.28*`, `7.29*`,
> `7.30*`; one, `7.27.2.3-mktime-normalizes`, predates this page).
> **rt** (clang + `runtime/`): 25 PASS, 5 DIFFER, 10 NOBUILD.
> **sh** (stage08 cc + the selfhost library): 25 PASS, 5 DIFFER, 10 NOBUILD.
> No TIMEOUT, no ORACLE-SPLIT left (six probes carry a `.expect` ruling).

The two libraries share `runtime/time_std.c` (mktime, asctime, ctime,
difftime, strftime), and gmtime/localtime are ports of one another
(`runtime/time_extra.c`, `selfhost/stage08/libc/posix_time.c`), so every
verdict in 7.27 came out the same for both. The five DIFFERs are all in
that shared file. Since d47d9f47 time_t is signed 64-bit in both, and
the probes find it right: gmtime, localtime (local mean time before 1883
included) and mktime agree with glibc from year 1 to year 292277026.
They also return null or -1 at the edge of `tm_year`'s int (gmtime and
localtime do; mktime does not, item 3). `<uchar.h>`, `<wctype.h>` and
the functions of `<wchar.h>` are missing in both, as the inventory said.
The wide-character probes are targets for whoever writes them.

Every leg runs with `TZ=America/Chicago` in the "C" locale.
`regression/libc-tests/time_conv.c`, `time_wide.c` and `time_zone.c` are
the older differential tests of the same code. They compare the libraries
with the host, so they share the host's choices. The probes here are
checked against the standard instead.

## Summary of gaps

**Wrong behaviour (DIFFER), both libraries, all in `runtime/time_std.c`**

1. **difftime overflows when the difference does not fit in time_t.**
   7.27.2.2p2 asks for `time1 - time0` as a double, and that difference
   need not be representable in time_t. Smallest case:
   `difftime(LLONG_MAX, -1)` should be 9223372036854775808 (2^63) and is
   **-9223372036854775808**. `difftime(LLONG_MAX, LLONG_MIN)` should be
   18446744073709551616 and is **-1**. The cause:
   `(double)((long long)later - (long long)earlier)` subtracts in
   long long, which overflows (that is undefined behaviour in the library
   itself). Converting each operand to double first is not exact either.
   glibc subtracts in a wider or unsigned type and handles the sign.
   (`7.27.2.2-difftime-extremes`)
2. **mktime ignores a tm_isdst that disagrees with the zone.**
   Footnote 320 to 7.27.2.3p2 says a positive tm_isdst makes mktime
   presume that DST is in effect, and zero that it is not. Both hosts
   apply that presumption. Smallest case: 2025-01-15 12:00 with
   `tm_isdst = 1` means 12:00 CDT, which is 11:00 CST, so the result
   should be **1736960400**, with the structure normalized to 11:00 and
   isdst=0. rt and sh return **1736964000** (12:00 CST), as if tm_isdst
   were -1. The mirror case is 2025-07-15 12:00 with `tm_isdst = 0`,
   which should be 1752602400 (13:00 CDT) and is 1752598800. The cause:
   after its first guess, mktime only looks for an instant one hour
   either way that shows the same wall-clock time with the requested
   flag (`c + zone_at(c) == local`). Such an instant exists only in the
   hour that repeats when the clocks go back. Anywhere else the request
   is dropped. The hosts instead convert the wall time with the offset
   the flag names. The repeated hour itself (2025-11-02 01:30, isdst 1
   and 0) is right. (`7.27.2.3-mktime-isdst`)
3. **mktime does not return -1 when the normalized year does not fit
   tm_year.** 7.27.2.3p2-p3: on success every member is brought into
   range, and a calendar time that cannot be represented gives
   `(time_t)-1`. Smallest case: `tm_year = INT_MAX, tm_mon = 12` (the
   year after the last one tm_year can hold) should return -1. rt and sh
   return a time value and leave the structure as it was passed. The
   same happens with `tm_year = INT_MIN, tm_mon = -1`, and with
   December 31 of year INT_MAX+1900 plus a day of seconds. The cause:
   mktime computes the seconds in long long and then calls
   `localtime_r(&result, tm)`, which returns NULL (EOVERFLOW) for that
   year. mktime ignores the NULL and returns `result`. Checking that
   return value would fix it. (`7.27.2.3-mktime-unrepresentable`)
4. **strftime `%y`, `%g` and `%C` for years before year 1.** These follow
   rulings (see below; neither host is right throughout).
   `%y` and `%g` are "the last 2 digits" (7.27.3.5p3), so year -1 gives
   **01**. rt and sh print **99**, because they reduce the year modulo
   100 toward the floor. Year -9 gives 09 (rt/sh: 91) and -1999 gives 99
   (rt/sh: 01). `%C` is the year divided by 100 *truncated* (7.27.3.5p3),
   so -1 to -99 give 00, -101 gives -1 and -1999 gives -19. rt and sh
   floor the quotient: -1, -2 and -20. The cause is
   `in_range((int)(year % 100), 100)` for `%y`/`%g`, and
   `(year - in_range(...)) / 100` for `%C`. They agree with glibc, whose
   formulas are the same. Low priority: only proleptic years at or below
   0 are affected. (`7.27.3.5-strftime-negative-year`)
5. *(Ruled not a defect, 2026-11-01.)* **strftime `%z` comes from
   `tm_gmtoff`, not from `tm_isdst`.** For a structure built from C's
   members alone (memset to zero, then every member 7.27.1p4 names),
   7.27.3.5p3 brackets `%z` with `[tm_isdst]`: in America/Chicago that
   reads as -0600 for tm_isdst=0 and -0500 for 1, which is what macOS
   prints.  rt, sh and glibc print +0000, because tm_gmtoff is 0.  The
   project follows POSIX.1-2024 here, which specifies tm_gmtoff for
   `%z`; every structure from localtime or mktime is right either way
   (`7.27.3.5-strftime-zone` passes). (`7.27.3.5-strftime-zone-isdst`)

**Rulings on oracle splits** (each has a `.expect`)

- `7.27.2.3-mktime-years`: macOS returns -1 for any local time before
  1901, which is a host limitation. The year-1, 1600 and 1850 lines are
  representable times (time_t is 64-bit), so 7.27.2.3p3 does not allow
  -1. glibc's values are right: they are UTC plus the zone's local mean
  time, -5:50:36, and year 1 begins on a Monday (proleptic Gregorian).
  The expect file is glibc's output. rt and sh pass.
- `7.27.3.5-strftime-century-year`: two conversions are split.
  - `%Y` for years below 1000: macOS zero-pads to four digits ("0999").
    7.27.3.5p3 says only "the year as a decimal number" and, unlike
    `%d` (01-31) or `%j` (001-366), gives no width, so the ruling is
    "999", as glibc prints.
  - `%C` for years below 1000: glibc drops the leading zero ("9"). The
    stated range (00-99) gives two digits, the same convention as `%d`
    and `%y`, so the ruling is "09", as macOS prints.

  rt and sh print the ruling and pass.
- `7.27.3.5-strftime-iso-year`: `%G` for week-based years below 1000.
  The ruling is the same as for `%Y`: unpadded ("999", "1"), glibc's
  form. rt and sh pass.
- `7.27.3.5-strftime-negative-year`: neither host follows the text
  throughout.
  - `%Y` and `%G` are unpadded with a sign ("-1"), as glibc prints.
    macOS prints "-001".
  - `%y` and `%g` are the last two digits of the year, without the sign:
    -1 gives "01". macOS does this; glibc gives "99".
  - `%C` truncates the quotient as C's integer division does (6.5.5p6):
    -1 to -99 give "00", -101 gives "-1". glibc floors ("-1", "-2").
    macOS truncates but keeps a sign on zero ("-0"), which C does not
    ask for.
- `7.27.3.5-strftime-zone-isdst`: glibc's output, by the POSIX ruling
  of item 5 (C's text, read strictly, gives macOS's).
- `7.28.1-mbrtoc16-c16rtomb`: macOS cannot build it (it has no
  `<uchar.h>`). glibc's output is what 7.28.1.1 and 7.28.1.2 give for
  ASCII in the "C" locale, and it is the expect file.

**Not probed, on purpose**

- **mktime with an out-of-range `tm_sec` across a DST change.** Both
  hosts add excess seconds as elapsed time after converting the other
  fields. For 2025-03-09 00:00 plus 10800 seconds they give 04:00 CDT;
  rt and sh give 03:00 CDT, normalizing the seconds into the wall-clock
  fields first. 7.27.2.3 does not say how normalization and DST
  interact, so neither answer is wrong. `7.27.2.3-mktime-fields` crosses
  DST with an out-of-range `tm_hour` instead, where all four agree.
- **asctime of a year below 1000 or above 9999, or of members out of
  range.** 7.27.3.1p3 makes these undefined. The probe stays within
  1000-9999 and checks the space-padded day field (" 1" in a 3-wide
  field after the month) and the length of 25 characters plus the
  newline.
- **`%Z`.** In the "C" locale it is implementation-defined (7.27.3.5p7).
  `7.27.3.5-strftime-zone` checks the CST/CDT abbreviations on localtime
  results, where both hosts agree.
- **`clock` and `time` values.** The probes check what is guaranteed:
  the `(time_t)-1` and `(clock_t)-1` conventions, that the value is also
  stored through the argument, and that it does not go backwards.
